The 14-Day Plan

None of this is set in stone. These are just ideas — skip stuff, sleep in, wander off. The green days have nothing planned on purpose.

Day 1 – Land in Tokyo ✈️
  • ✈️ Land at Narita. Grab a Suica/Pasmo card at the airport — you'll tap it for everything.
  • 🚄 Narita Express or Skyliner into the city (~60–90 min, ~$25–$30).
  • 🏨 Check in, drop the bags, freshen up.
  • 🍜 Easy first dinner — gyudon, ramen, or conveyor sushi near the hotel.
  • 🛍️ 7-Eleven run for snacks.
  • 😴 Get sleep. Jet lag is real, don't fight it tonight.
Day 2 – Shibuya & Harajuku
  • ⛩️ Meiji Jingu Shrine — a calm walk through the forest, easy on the legs.
  • 🛍️ Harajuku's Takeshita Street — crepes and weird, fun fashion.
  • 🚶 Omotesando — tree-lined street, all the big brand stores.
  • 🌃 Shibuya Crossing — watch the chaos from Mag's Park or a Starbucks window.
  • 🍦 Grab matcha soft serve or a taiyaki.
  • 🍶 Izakaya dinner down a Shibuya back alley.
Day 3 – Akihabara & Ueno
  • 🤖 Akihabara — anime, manga, and electronics everywhere (Animate, Yodobashi, Radio Kaikan).
  • 🎮 Multi-floor arcades — Sega, Taito, Club Sega.
  • ⛩️ Ueno Park — temples, museums, even a zoo.
  • 🛍️ Ameya Yokocho — a market street for snacks and cheap clothes.
  • 🍣 Conveyor sushi or tempura for dinner.
Day 4 – Asakusa & Odaiba
  • ⛩️ Senso-ji Temple — the oldest one in Tokyo. Walk down Nakamise shopping street on the way in.
  • 🚢 Walk (or take a short boat ride) along the Sumida River.
  • 🗼 Tokyo Skytree — great views, plus a mall underneath.
  • 🌇 Odaiba — waterfront area, TeamLab or the giant Gundam statue.
  • 🍡 Grab dango or taiyaki from a street stall.
Day 5 – TeamLab & Ginza
  • 🎨 TeamLab Planets or Borderless — book tickets online ahead of time, they sell out.
  • 🛍️ Ginza — the Uniqlo flagship, stationery shops, good window shopping.
  • 🌳 Hamarikyu Gardens — a peaceful traditional garden with a teahouse.
  • 🍱 Tsukiji Outer Market for a fresh sushi breakfast or lunch.
Day 6 – Kamakura Day Trip
  • 🚃 Take the JR Yokosuka Line out to Kamakura (~1 hr, ~$5).
  • 🕉️ The Great Buddha (Daibutsu) — a huge bronze statue that just sits outside in the open.
  • ⛩️ Hasedera Temple — nice garden, hydrangeas are blooming in June.
  • 🥾 Short, easy hike from the Buddha to Hase (~20 min, gentle trail).
  • 🍤 Shirasu don (whitebait rice bowl) for lunch — it's a local specialty.
  • 🚃 Head back to Tokyo by evening.
Day 7 – Open Day / Hike If You Want

Nothing planned. Sleep in, wander, or pick something below.

  • ⛰️ Mt. Takao: about 1 hr from Shinjuku. A moderate hike, a temple at the top, and you can see Fuji on a clear day.
  • 🏙️ More of Shinjuku: free views from the Tokyo Metropolitan building, a walk through Kabukicho's neon, or Omoide Yokocho's alleys.
  • 🛍️ Ikebukuro: anime shops and Sunshine City.
  • 🛍️ Nakano: Nakano Broadway for vintage manga and collectibles.
  • 🎬 Studio Ghibli Museum: needs tickets bought way in advance, so plan ahead if you want to go.
Day 8 – Night Walks & Views
  • 🏙️ Shinjuku — free sunset views from the Metropolitan Government building.
  • 🌃 Kabukicho — neon everywhere, the Godzilla head, totally fine to just walk through.
  • 🍜 Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane") — tiny yakitori stalls, cheap beer.
  • 🍸 Golden Gai — a maze of tiny bars, good for one drink and people-watching.
  • 🍰 Dessert — Japanese cheesecake or a matcha parfait.
Day 9 – Shopping & Last Day in Tokyo
  • 🛍️ Morning: last anime/tech buys in Akihabara (a lot of it's tax-free).
  • 🛍️ Afternoon: clothes in Shibuya/Shinjuku, Don Quijote for souvenirs.
  • 🎮 One more arcade run if anyone's got energy left.
  • 🍱 Farewell izakaya dinner in Tokyo.
Day 10 – Tokyo → Osaka
  • 🚄 Bullet train to Shin-Osaka (~2.5 hr, ~$130 — reserve your seat ahead).
  • 🏨 Check in to the Osaka hotel (Namba or Umeda).
  • 🛍️ Quick walk around the neighborhood, convenience store run.
  • 🌃 Evening in Dotonbori — neon everywhere, takoyaki, okonomiyaki.
  • 🛍️ Shinsaibashi shopping street is right nearby too.
Day 11 – Osaka Classics
  • 🏯 Osaka Castle — museum inside, and the park around it is nice too.
  • 🍢 Shinsekai — an old-school neighborhood, good spot for kushikatsu.
  • 🐟 Kuromon Ichiba Market — fresh sushi, tamagoyaki, fruit.
  • 🏙️ Umeda Sky Building — the floating garden observatory has great views.
  • 🍮 Dessert: matcha pudding or taiyaki.
Day 12 – Anime, Tech & Night Walk
  • 🤖 Den Den Town (Nipponbashi) — Osaka's version of Akihabara.
  • 🛍️ Namba Parks, or check out the NMB48 area.
  • 🏮 Hozenji Yokocho — a quiet, lantern-lit stone alley, nice for a night walk.
  • 🍧 Late-night kakigori (shaved ice) or taiyaki.
  • 🛍️ Don Quijote Namba for cheap souvenirs and snacks.
Day 13 – Kyoto Day Trip
  • 🚄 Rapid train to Kyoto (~30 min, ~$4).
  • ⛩️ Fushimi Inari — hike up through the endless red torii gates (~1–2 hrs).
  • ⛩️ Kiyomizu-dera — a wooden temple with a great view over the valley.
  • 🚶 Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka streets — old shops, tea houses.
  • 🍵 Matcha and sweets in the Higashiyama district.
  • 🚄 Back to Osaka by evening.
Day 14 – Kobe/Himeji or Free Day / Fly Home

Last day, keep it flexible. Pick whatever sounds best.

  • 🏯 Option A – Himeji: ~45 min away. The "White Heron" castle. Easy to get to KIX from there.
  • 🐄 Option B – Kobe: ~30 min away. Harborland, Kitano, and Kobe beef for lunch.
  • 🛍️ Option C – Stay in Osaka: last-minute souvenir run in Shinsaibashi.
  • ✈️ Head to Kansai Airport (KIX) via the Haruka train (~45 min).
  • 🛫 Fly KIX → SFO, nonstop.

Pack List

A pretty standard June packing list. Travel light — coin lockers at stations are only about $5.

📄 Documents & Money

  • Passports (valid through March 2028)
  • Printouts of hotel & flight confirmations
  • Travel insurance card
  • Yen cash ($500–800/person)
  • A credit card with no foreign transaction fee

📱 Electronics

  • Phone + charger
  • Portable battery (max 20,000 mAh for carry-on)
  • USB-C or Lightning cables
  • Earphones / earbuds

👕 Clothing

  • Compact umbrella
  • Light rain jacket / poncho
  • 5–7 quick-dry t-shirts
  • 2 pants/shorts
  • 1 nicer outfit for an izakaya dinner
  • Comfortable, broken-in sneakers
  • Sandals / slides
  • Socks & underwear (7+ pairs)

🧴 Toiletries & Health

  • Travel-size everything
  • Hand sanitizer
  • Band-aids / blister pads
  • Basic meds (pain, allergies, stomach)
  • Sunscreen

🎒 Misc Gear

  • Foldable tote bag (for souvenirs)
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Sunglasses
  • Small daypack for wandering around
  • Travel pillow / eye mask for the flight

🇯🇵 Japan Specifics

  • Type-A plug adapter if you need one (most US plugs just fit)
  • Shoes that slip on/off easily — you'll take them off a lot at temples and some places to eat
  • Have your travel apps and QR codes set up before you leave

Good to Know

🌤️ Weather & Clothing

June in Tokyo/Osaka runs 70–85°F, humid, with afternoon rain most days. Quick-dry clothes, an umbrella, and waterproof shoes will save you. It can feel cool in the evenings after it rains.

🚄 Getting Around

  • Buy a Suica or Pasmo card at the airport — tap it everywhere.
  • For the bullet train, reserve your seat and buy the ticket at the station.
  • Use Google Maps or a train app for exact times.
  • A JR Pass isn't worth it for the route we're doing.

📶 Staying Connected

  • Pocket WiFi: about $8/day, and we can all share one.
  • eSIM: Ubigi or Airalo — no need to swap a physical SIM.
  • Coverage in the cities is great either way.

💬 Phrases Worth Knowing

  • Sumimasen — excuse me / sorry
  • Arigato gozaimasu — thank you
  • Eigo ga hanasemasu ka? — do you speak English?
  • Toire wa doko desu ka? — where's the bathroom?
  • Kore o kudasai — I'll have this, please

📲 Apps to Download

  • Google Maps — for trains, walking, restaurant hours.
  • Google Translate — the camera mode is great for menus.
  • A train time app — for exact departure times.
  • Tabelog — local restaurant reviews.

💴 Money Stuff

  • Cash is king at markets and small stalls.
  • 7-Eleven ATMs will take foreign cards.
  • Use Splitwise or Venmo to settle up on hotel/food.
  • A lot of small places are still cash-only.

👥 If We Split Up

  • Pick a landmark to meet at if the group splits.
  • Leave a note on the hotel door if you're last one out.
  • A station clock or a big statue works as a backup meeting spot.
  • Share your live location if you're running late.

🙇 A Few Manners Things

  • No tipping — it's actually considered rude, people will try to give it back.
  • Shoes off indoors — hostels, temples, traditional inns.
  • Stand on the left on escalators in Tokyo, right in Osaka.
  • Keep it quiet on trains, no phone calls.
  • At temples: wash your hands at the water basin, and don't take photos of people mid-prayer.

☔ If It's Raining

  • TeamLab — indoor digital art, book ahead.
  • Underground malls like Crystal City or Umeda.
  • Don Quijote — endless floors of random stuff to look at.
  • Arcades (Sega, Taito) — fully indoor.
  • Covered shopping streets like Shinsaibashi.
